\section{Simulation}

\subsection{Noise and PSF}

Our simulation is divided into $2500$ sub-samples and each sub-sample contains $10^4$ postage-stamps with
$64\times 64$ pixels. The pixel size is set to $0.168$ arcesc. The noise property and PSF are the same for
galaxies in one sub-sample while they vary among different sub-samples. The HSC pipeline reconstruct the PSF
models and noise variances for any position on the coadd images. We collect $2500$ noise variances and PSF
models from a set of random positions on the coadd images of HSC-WIDE layer within the footprint of the HSC
shape catalog.

Noise on the coadd images has spacial correlation due to the warping kernel used to resample single exposures
on the common pixel frame in the coadding process. We sample the noise correlations from the blank pixels on
the coadd images. The sampled noise correlations, which are noisy at individual level, are subsequently divided
into eight groups and stacked in each group to create eight different noise correlations with high signal to
noise ratio.

Each sub-sample is assigned with a noise PSF model, noise variance and noise correlation function. We then reweight
each subsample to match the seeing histograms and noise variance histograms of the simulation to the observation.
To note, the HSC WIDE layer is divided six observational fields (XMM, GAMA09H, WIDE12H, GAMA15H, VVDS, HECTOMAP)
and the reweighting is conducted for each field. The seeing histograms and noise variance histograms of the
observation and simulation are shown in Figure \ref{fig:noiHist} and Figure \ref{fig:fwhmHist}.

\subsection{Galaxies}
We use the galaxy training sample created by \citet{HSC1-GREAT3Sim} as our input galaxies. \citet{HSC1-GREAT3Sim}
selects galaxies from the HSC WIDE-DEPTH coadds \citep{HSC1-data} with CModel magnitude less than $25.2$.
Subsequently, \citet{HSC1-GREAT3Sim} finds the centers of these selected galaxies on the COSMOS-HST
\citep{HST-ACSpipe} exposures and cuts out postage-stamps around these positions. These positions are set
to as the centers of the postage-stamps and the postage sizes are required to be larger than $10.75$ arcsec
to contain the galaxy light and structures of neighboring sources.

\citet{HSC1-GREAT3Sim} creates three training samples from three WIDE-DEPTH coadds with different seeing values which are
0.5\arcsec, 0.7\arcsec and 1.0\arcsec. We use the training sample with the best seeing as our input galaxy sample since it
has the largest number of galaxies.

\galsim \citep{Galsim}, which is an open-source package for galaxy image simulation, is used to simulate the HSC-like
postage-stamps from the COSMOS HST postage-stamp. The PSF is deconvolved from each input postage-stamp and then the
postage-stamp is convolved with an assigned HSC PSF model. The original noise is whitened for each postage stamp
then a noise with an assigned HSC variance and HSC nosie correlation function is added to the postage-stamp.

\subsection{Image Processing}
HSC pipeline is being developed to process the data of HSC survey and will finally be applied to LSST survey. We use HSC
pipeline to detect galaxies from the simulated images and isolate blended galaxies with HSC deblender to define a sample
of sources. Subsequently, the HSC pipeline is used to perform magnitude and shape measurements on the detected sources.

\subsection{Selection Bias}
Selection bias refers to the multiplicative bias or additive bias caused by selection. Such bias emerges 
if the observable used for selection correlates with the shear signal or the ellipticities of PSF. We denote 
the observable used for selection as $X$ and the correlation with the shear or PSF ellipticities has the form
\begin{equation}
X=\bar{X}+g_1Y_1+e_{\rm{PSF,1}}Z_1+....
\end{equation}
Here $\bar{X}$ refers to the intrinsic property before the shear distortion and PSF smearing, $Y$ refers to the
response of the property $\bar{X}$ to the shear which is a spin-$2$ property, and $Z$ quantifies the correlation
between the observed property $X$ and the shape of PSF which is caused by the imperfect PSF revision in the 
observation of $X$. We follow \citet{HSC1-GREAT3Sim} and give out the changes in the first component of ellipticities
due to the selection
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Delta \left\langle e_1\right \rangle  &= g_1\int \frac{dn}{Nd\Omega } |_{X=X_{\rm{edge}}} Y_1 e_1 d\Omega \\
        &+ e_{\rm{PSF,1}} \int \frac{dn}{Nd\Omega}|_{X=X_{\rm{edge}}} Z_1 e_1 d\Omega.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Then with the assumption that
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 \frac{dn}{Nd\Omega}&=p_a(X)p_b(\Omega')\\
\end{split}
\end{equation}
we have
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Delta \left\langle e_1\right \rangle  &= g_1p_a(X_{\rm{edge}})\int p_b Y_1 e_1 d\Omega'|_{X=X_{\rm{edge}}} \\
        &+ e_{\rm{PSF,1}} p_a(X_{\rm{edge}}) \int p_b Z_1 e_1 d\Omega'|_{X=X_{\rm{edge}}} .
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The multiplicative selection bias and additive selection bias is defined as 
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
m_{\rm{sel}}&=p_a(X_{\rm{edge}})\int p_b Y_1 e_1 d\Omega'|_{X=X_{\rm{edge}}} \\
            &=p_a(X_{\rm{edge}}) A_m,\\
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
a_{\rm{sel}}&=p_a(X_{\rm{edge}}) \int p_b Z_1 e_1 d\Omega'|_{X=X_{\rm{edge}}} \\
            &=p_a(X_{\rm{edge}}) A_a,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
respectively. We denote the ratio between the multiplicative (additive) bias and partial density function 
at $X_{\rm{edge}}$ as $A_{m}$ ($A_{a}$).
